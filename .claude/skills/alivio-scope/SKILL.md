# Draft a SOW

The SOW is the document every later dispute resolves against. Scoping from a
qualification call rather than a real discovery is guessing — say so if that is
what you have been given.

## Steps

### 1. Deliverables, countable

Not "a marketing site" but "5 pages: home, services, about, careers, contact —
responsive, CMS-editable." **If you cannot count it, nobody can tell when it is
done** — including the person building it.

### 2. Exclusions — this matters more than the deliverables

Where "obviously implied" goes to be made explicit. Always include:

- Content and copy, unless named as a deliverable
- Photography, illustration, licensed assets
- Hosting, domains, third-party subscription costs
- Integrations with systems not named
- Maintenance or support after handoff
- Training beyond the handoff session
- Work for anyone other than the named decision-maker

Plus anything discussed in discovery and ruled out. **Name it explicitly** —
that is exactly where a scope argument starts three weeks later.

### 3. Acceptance criteria, one checkable sentence each

"Done when the form submits and the lead appears in the client's CRM" is
checkable. "Done when the site looks professional" is a future argument.

### 4. Client obligations, with owners and dates

Most slipped projects are waiting on something the client owes. List each,
name an owner, give it a date. Include the delay clause verbatim from
`01-delivery/scoping-and-sow.md`.

### 5. Price it — flag, do not decide

Use `02-sales/proposal-and-pricing.md`. Estimate hours honestly, **add 30%** for
review cycles and coordination, check against the floor. Under the floor is a
scope problem, not a price problem — reduce scope rather than the number.

**Present the number as a proposal to Joel, never as a decision.** Joel prices.

### 6. Fill the template

Copy `05-templates/sow-template.md` and complete every `[BRACKET]`.

## What you will hit, every time

Two gaps make parts of the SOW uncompletable today. **Flag them; do not invent
values:**

- **There is no hourly rate.** `[$RATE]/hour` in section 6 and in the change
  order has no value, which makes the change-order mechanism unusable as
  written. Joel must set it.
- **Pricing floors are inferred**, from RLTRS at $29,000 and a published
  $5–10K install figure. They have not been confirmed. Anything quoted from them
  carries that risk.

## Hard rules

- **Never send a SOW.** Draft; Joel reviews, prices, and signs.
- **Never promise a date** that has not been confirmed with whoever will do the
  work.
- **Two revision rounds.** Always. It appears in seven documents and changing it
  is a coordinated edit, not a per-deal negotiation.
- **Never promise a business result** — traffic, ranking, revenue, conversion.
  Alivio promises work, not outcomes it does not control.
- **No work starts before signature and deposit received.** Both.
