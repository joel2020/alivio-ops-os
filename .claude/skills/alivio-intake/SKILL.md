# Qualify a lead

Produce a **go / no-go with reasoning**, not a summary of the enquiry.

## Steps

### 1. Run the disqualifier screen first

Any single hit is sufficient to decline. From
`02-sales/icp-and-disqualifiers.md`:

- No named decision-maker, or a committee with no owner
- Budget under $5,000 for project work
- Timeline under 3 weeks for anything substantial
- Wants spec work, a free pilot, or payment in exposure
- Cannot articulate the outcome — wants "a website" with no reason attached
- Rescue work without a paid audit first
- Requires on-site presence or incompatible synchronous hours
- Wants Alivio inside tooling where the work cannot be inspected or handed over
- Asked for a discount before hearing the price
- Was rude to Joel, or about a previous vendor, in the first conversation
- Regulated work Alivio is not qualified for, without the client owning compliance
- Anything Alivio could not say the name of afterwards

**Say which disqualifier fired.** "Not a fit" is not a reason.

### 2. If nothing fired, run the three-question screen

1. Is there one person who can say yes?
2. Is there a number attached to the problem?
3. Would Alivio want this in the portfolio?

### 3. Check the positive signals

Operator-owner with budget authority · 5–100 people · a problem with a number ·
$8K+ project or $2K+/month retainer · 4+ week timeline · comfortable async ·
has already tried something.

Referral, modern tooling, asks price early, names their decision-maker
unprompted, talks about outcomes — all move faster.

### 4. Produce the verdict

```
VERDICT: go / no-go / need more information

Why: <the specific reason — a disqualifier by name, or the strongest signal>

Service line: AI build / web / design / marketing
Rough band: <from 02-sales/proposal-and-pricing.md — floors are hard>
What I do not know: <the gaps that would change the answer>

Next action: <book a 30-min qualification call / decline / send the two questions>
```

### 5. If unclear, send the two questions rather than guessing

- "What's the outcome you need, and what happens if it doesn't land?"
- "Who else needs to say yes, and what's your timeline?"

The first separates a real problem from a wish. The second surfaces committees
and fake urgency before a call is spent on them.

### 6. Log it

Every lead becomes an issue in the Linear pipeline project within 1 business
day, whatever channel it arrived through. **A lead that exists only in an inbox
does not exist.** On a decline, log which disqualifier applied — the quarterly
read of decline reasons is how the ICP gets corrected.

## Hard rules

- **Never quote a price.** Bands are for Joel's internal sizing. Only Joel
  quotes, and Alivio's real floors are still unconfirmed — see the assumptions
  in `02-sales/proposal-and-pricing.md`.
- **Never send the decline.** Draft it; Joel sends.
- **Declining well is a good outcome.** Capacity is the scarce resource at 4–6
  concurrent clients — one wrong fit consumes a fifth of it for two months.
- **Do not soften a disqualifier** because the lead is exciting. Repeated
  exceptions mean the list is wrong, and that is a decision to make deliberately
  rather than one at a time.
