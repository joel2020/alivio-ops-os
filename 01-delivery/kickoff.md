# Kickoff

**Owner:** PM · **Trigger:** Project enters stage 2 (SOW signed, deposit received, Linear set up) · **Cadence:** Once per project · **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-26

## Purpose

The meeting where the PM takes ownership in front of the client, and where every
assumption that would otherwise surface in week four gets surfaced in week one.

What breaks without it: the client keeps treating Joel as the contact, which
defeats the entire point of a PM and makes "Joel off for two weeks" impossible.

Kickoff is also the last cheap moment to discover that the client's decision-maker
is not who the SOW says, or that a promised input does not exist.

## Steps

**Before — PM, 30 minutes**

1. Read the SOW end to end. Not skimmed.
2. Confirm Linear has every milestone with dates matching the SOW.
3. Confirm contractors are booked with committed dates.
4. Create the channel of record and invite the client.
5. Send the agenda 24 hours ahead, using `../05-templates/kickoff-agenda.md`.

**During — 45–60 minutes, PM runs it, Joel attends the first 10 minutes**

6. **Joel hands over explicitly, out loud:** *"[PM] owns this from here. They'll be
   your day-to-day contact. I'm still on the account and you'll see me at
   milestones."* This sentence is the whole reason Joel attends. Without it the
   client keeps emailing Joel, and every later attempt to redirect reads as a
   downgrade.
7. Walk the deliverables list and the exclusions list. Both. Out loud.
8. Confirm the decision-maker by name, and confirm they are on the call.
9. Confirm the client's inputs, owners, and dates. Write them into Linear live,
   on screen, so they are real.
10. Agree the channel of record and the SLAs, both directions.
11. Walk the timeline and name the checkpoints.
12. State the revision policy: two rounds per deliverable, and what counts as a
    round.
13. State the delay clause once, warmly.
14. Agree one sentence describing what success looks like. In the client's words.
    Write it down and read it back.

**After — PM, same day**

15. Notes into Obsidian; decisions promoted to the decision log.
16. Written recap in the channel of record: decisions, owners, dates, the success
    sentence. **Same day.** A recap that arrives Thursday for a Monday kickoff has
    already let two ambiguities harden.
17. Move the project to Build once every kickoff exit criterion is met.

## The four questions that prevent most later pain

Ask them even when the answer seems obvious:

1. **"Who else, besides you, could stop this from shipping?"** Finds the hidden
   stakeholder while it costs nothing.
2. **"What would make this a failure for you, even if we delivered everything in
   the SOW?"** Finds the unwritten success criterion.
3. **"What's your turnaround when we send something for review?"** Turns the
   feedback SLA into a mutual commitment rather than an Alivio complaint later.
4. **"What has gone wrong for you with agencies before?"** Tells you exactly what
   this client is watching for.

## Definition of done

Every exit criterion in `project-lifecycle.md` stage 2 is checked, the recap is
sent, and the client has said the PM's name back to you.

## Decision rights

- **PM decides alone:** how the meeting runs, what goes in the recap.
- **Joel decides alone:** whether to accept a client input date that puts the
  timeline at risk.
- **Escalate when:** the decision-maker is not who the SOW names, a promised input
  does not exist, or the client describes a scope different from the SOW. All three
  are pre-Build problems and cheap now, expensive later.

## Assumptions

- Assumes kickoff is a live call. Async kickoff is possible for small engagements
  but the handover sentence has to happen synchronously or it does not land.
- Assumes Joel is willing to hand over in front of the client. **If Joel would
  rather stay primary contact, that is a legitimate choice — but it should be
  decided deliberately**, because it is the load-bearing assumption under the
  90-day goal.
