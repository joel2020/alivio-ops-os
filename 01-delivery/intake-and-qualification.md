# Intake and Qualification

**Owner:** Joel (Principal) · **Trigger:** A new inbound lead lands anywhere · **Cadence:** Within 1 business day of arrival · **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-26

## Purpose

Decide fast whether a lead is worth Alivio's time, and get the ones that are into
a scheduled discovery before they cool. Speed is most of the win rate here — a
lead answered in a day converts far better than the same lead answered in four,
and Alivio is competing against whoever replied first.

What breaks without it: good leads go cold in an inbox while bad ones consume a
discovery call that was never going to close.

## Steps

1. **Capture, within 1 business day.** Every lead gets a Linear issue in the
   pipeline project, whatever channel it arrived through — email, referral,
   WhatsApp, LinkedIn, a form. **A lead that exists only in an inbox does not
   exist.**

2. **Record, at minimum:** name, company, how they found Alivio, what they said
   they want, and the date. Missing fields are chased at the Friday pipeline
   hygiene sweep.

3. **Run the ICP screen** from `../02-sales/icp-and-disqualifiers.md`. This is a
   read, not a conversation — most leads can be screened from what they already
   wrote.

4. **Route:**

   | Outcome | Action |
   |---|---|
   | **Clear disqualifier** | Decline within 1 business day, politely, with a referral if one is obvious. Log the reason. |
   | **Clear fit** | Book a 30-minute qualification call within 5 business days |
   | **Unclear** | One clarifying email — the two questions below — then re-screen |

5. **The two clarifying questions** that resolve most ambiguity:
   - "What's the outcome you need, and what happens if it doesn't land?"
   - "Who else needs to say yes, and what's your timeline?"

   The first separates a real problem from a wish. The second surfaces committees
   and fake urgency before a call is spent on them.

6. **Qualification call, 30 minutes.** Not discovery. The purpose is deciding
   whether to invest in discovery. Cover:
   - [ ] What they are trying to achieve, in their words
   - [ ] Why now, and what happens if it slips
   - [ ] Who decides, and who else must agree
   - [ ] Budget range — asked directly, see below
   - [ ] Timeline, and whether it is theirs or imposed
   - [ ] What they have already tried

7. **Ask about budget on the qualification call.** Directly: *"Most engagements
   like this land between $8K and $30K depending on scope — is that the range
   you're working in?"* A prospect who cannot answer is not qualified yet.
   Discovering a $3K budget after a two-hour discovery is a self-inflicted wound.

8. **Decide within 1 business day**, go or no-go, and log the reasoning. Not
   deciding is the expensive option — it keeps the lead warm in your head and cold
   in reality.

9. **On go:** move to Discovery, schedule it, and send the discovery prep note.
   **On no-go:** decline in writing within 1 business day, log the reason against
   the disqualifier that applied.

## Decision rights

- **Joel decides alone:** every go/no-go, and any exception to a disqualifier.
- **PM decides alone:** nothing at this stage. Intake is entirely Joel's until
  there is a second salesperson.
- **Escalate when:** n/a — this is already the top.

## Definition of done

Every lead has a logged decision within 1 business day of the qualification call,
and no lead sits uncontacted for more than 1 business day after arriving.

## Assumptions

- **The $8K–$30K range quoted in step 7 is derived, not confirmed** — from the
  RLTRS engagement at $29,000 and Alivio's published $5–10K install pricing. Joel
  must confirm before anyone says it on a call.
- Assumes leads are low enough in volume that Joel personally screens each. True
  at 4–6 concurrent clients; past roughly 10 leads a week it needs a form that
  screens before it reaches him.
- Assumes intake is a Linear project. If Joel prefers a separate CRM later, only
  this file and `crm-hygiene.md` change.
