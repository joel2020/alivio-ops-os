# Project Red Escalation

**Owner:** Joel (Principal) · **Trigger:** Any RED criterion is met · **Cadence:** Same day, always · **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-26

## Purpose

Projects rarely fail suddenly. They fail slowly while everyone hopes the next week
will be better, and the person who could have fixed it finds out too late to fix
it cheaply. This document removes the hoping.

What breaks without it: Joel discovers a red project when the client escalates,
which is the most expensive moment to find out and the one that costs the
relationship rather than just the margin.

## The RED triggers

Any **one** of these. Not a judgment call, not a discussion.

- Milestone slipped more than **5 business days**
- Client unresponsive more than **7 calendar days** on something blocking
- **Two consecutive** missed checkpoints
- Scope is **disputed** — client believes something is included that Alivio does not
- An invoice for this project is more than **30 days** overdue
- A contractor will miss a committed date and there is no cover
- Anyone on the team says they think the project is in trouble

That last one is deliberate and matters most. **A person's instinct that something
is wrong is a valid trigger and needs no evidence.** Requiring proof before raising
a concern is how concerns stop being raised.

## Steps

1. **PM raises it to Joel the same day.** Not at the Wednesday roll-up. Same day.
   Message, not email.

2. **PM writes the red note** — 10 minutes, five lines, before any meeting:
   - Which trigger fired, and when
   - What is actually happening, factually
   - What it puts at risk: money, date, relationship, or all three
   - What the PM has already tried
   - What the PM recommends

   Recommending something is required. "I don't know what to do" is fine as a
   sixth line, but the attempt has to be on the page — it is what turns a problem
   into a decision.

3. **Joel and PM meet within 1 business day.** 30 minutes, decision required at
   the end. A red project that stays red without a decision is now two problems.

4. **Pick one, explicitly:**

   | Option | When it is right |
   |---|---|
   | **Absorb** | Small overrun, good client, worth the goodwill. Log the cost — absorbed cost that is never counted becomes invisible margin loss. |
   | **Re-scope** | The work changed. Change order, client signs, dates move. |
   | **Re-sequence** | The work is fine, the order is wrong. Move milestones, tell the client. |
   | **Add capacity** | Deadline is real and worth paying for. Joel approves the cost. |
   | **Reset with the client** | Expectations diverged. Joel leads that call, not the PM. |
   | **Stop** | Rare, and always Joel's. Sometimes the right answer, and much cheaper in month two than month five. |

5. **Tell the client the same day when the red is client-visible.** A slipped date
   the client will notice is told, not managed. Clients forgive slippage they hear
   about early and remember slippage they discover.

6. **Log the decision** in the decision log with the reasoning. Six months later
   the reasoning is the valuable part.

7. **Review daily until green or amber.** A red project gets a daily check-in
   between PM and Joel — five minutes, standing — until it is not red. This is the
   only daily ritual in the whole OS, and it exists because red is the only state
   that justifies one.

## What never happens

- Waiting for the Wednesday roll-up to raise a red
- Telling the client after the milestone has already been missed
- Letting a red project stay red for two weeks without a decision
- Blaming a contractor to the client
- Fixing it by quietly working weekends. That hides the signal, burns the person,
  and guarantees a repeat.

## Decision rights

- **PM decides alone:** declaring red. Never questioned, never requires evidence.
  A false red costs 30 minutes; a missed red costs a client.
- **Joel decides alone:** which option is taken, whether to stop, anything with
  money attached, and any client reset conversation.
- **Escalate when:** it is already at Joel. This is the top.

## Definition of done

The project is amber or green, or has been deliberately stopped, and the decision
is in the log with its reasoning.

## Assumptions

- **The 5-day / 7-day / 30-day thresholds are best-practice guesses** calibrated
  for 4–8 week engagements. On a 4-week project a 5-day slip is over 10% of the
  timeline and may be too slow a trigger. Worth revisiting after a quarter.
- Assumes Joel is reachable within 1 business day. **During the two-week absence
  the 90-day goal describes, this document needs a named deputy** — currently
  undefined, and the sharpest unresolved dependency in the OS.
