# Change Order Template

**Owner:** PM drafts · Joel approves · **Trigger:** Scope change over $2,500, or any client-visible date change · **Cadence:** As needed · **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-26

## Purpose

Turn a scope change into a signed document before the work starts. Thresholds and
routing are in `../01-delivery/scope-change-protocol.md`; this is the artifact for
the top tier.

**No work begins before signature.** Starting is the approval, whatever was said.

## How to use it

1. **Do not start the work.** Not even a little.
2. Size it honestly, then round up. An estimate that is generous is a good outcome;
   one that is short is unbillable.
3. **Always state the date impact**, even when it is zero — "no change to the
   delivery date" is reassuring and prevents a later argument.
4. Send it as its own document. Never bury a change in an email thread.
5. On signature: invoice immediately, add to Linear, start.
6. Log it against the project so the total is visible in the weekly status report.

## The conversation that goes with it

A change order arriving without warning reads as opportunism. Say it out loud
first:

> "That's a good idea and it's outside what we scoped. It'd be [$AMOUNT] and
> move delivery to [DATE]. Want me to write it up?"

Named clearly, with a price and a date, most clients say yes. The ones who say no
have saved everyone a misunderstanding.

## Decision rights

- **PM decides alone:** drafting, and the effort estimate.
- **Joel decides alone:** the price, whether to waive it, whether to accept the
  change at all.
- **Escalate when:** the client believes the change was always in scope. That is a
  Joel conversation, not a PM one.

## Definition of done

Signed before work starts, invoiced on signature, logged against the project.

## Assumptions

- **Alivio does not bill hourly** (Joel, 2026-07-27), so a change order is a
  mini fixed-scope quote. Estimate the effort internally, add the same buffer the
  main pricing uses, and quote one number.
- A fixed price on a change order means a short estimate cannot be recovered
  later. Size generously — the client is agreeing to a number, not to a meter.
