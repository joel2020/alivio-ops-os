# Tax and Compliance Calendar

**Owner:** Joel (Principal) · **Trigger:** Calendar dates below · **Cadence:** Quarterly and annual · **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-26

## Purpose

Set money aside as it is earned and collect contractor paperwork as people are
onboarded, so tax deadlines are transfers rather than emergencies.

What breaks without it: a quarterly tax bill arrives against money already spent,
and January becomes a scramble to chase W-9s from contractors who worked in
February and may not respond.

**The core discipline: tax money is not Alivio's money.** It is collected on behalf
of a government and happens to be sitting in Alivio's account.

## The set-aside rule

**Move a fixed percentage of every payment received into a separate tax account,
on receipt.** Not monthly, not quarterly. On receipt.

The percentage must be set with an accountant — it depends on entity type, state,
and income. A common starting point for a US pass-through is **25–30%**, but
treating that as advice would be a mistake.

The separate account matters more than the exact percentage. Money that is visible
in the operating balance gets spent, regardless of what it was earmarked for.

## Quarterly

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Quarter start | Confirm the set-aside balance covers the estimate |
| Quarter start | Make the estimated payment |
| Quarter start | Review the set-aside percentage against actual income |

Estimated payment deadlines vary by jurisdiction; the accountant sets them and they
go in Joel's calendar with a two-week warning, not a two-day one.

## Annual

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **December** | Confirm a W-9 (or W-8BEN) exists for every contractor paid this year |
| **December** | Total payments per contractor; flag anyone over the 1099 threshold |
| **January** | Issue 1099s |
| **January** | File contractor forms with the tax authority |
| **January** | Renew insurance; review coverage against work actually being done |
| **January** | Review tool subscriptions and contracts |
| **Annual** | Confirm the entity is in good standing; file annual reports |

## Preventing the January scramble

The scramble happens because paperwork is collected at year end from people who
stopped working in spring. Two rules prevent it entirely:

1. **W-9 before first payment.** Enforced in `contractor-payments.md` onboarding.
   No form, no payment. This single rule eliminates most of the problem.
2. **December check, not January.** The December step catches a missing form while
   there is still a month to chase it.

## Records to retain

- Signed contractor agreements and tax forms
- Every invoice issued and received
- Bank statements and reconciliations
- Expense receipts
- Signed SOWs and change orders

Retention period is set by jurisdiction; the accountant advises. **Digital, backed
up, and not solely on one laptop** — a rule that applies to the business's records
as much as to its code.

## Decision rights

- **Joel decides alone:** all of it, in consultation with an accountant.
- **Escalate when:** n/a — though "escalate to the accountant" is the right instinct
  for anything on this page.

## Definition of done

The set-aside balance covers the current estimate, every contractor paid this year
has a tax form on file, and the annual dates are in Joel's calendar with warnings.

## Assumptions

- **This document assumes a US context and is not tax advice.** Entity type, state,
  and residency all change it materially. Every number here must be confirmed by an
  accountant before it is relied upon.
- **International contractors are likely given Alivio's Colombia-based work**, which
  changes the forms (W-8BEN, not W-9) and possibly introduces withholding
  obligations. Explicitly out of scope for this document and needs professional
  advice.
- **No accountant is named in the stack.** Having one is a prerequisite for this
  document to be more than a reminder to get one.
