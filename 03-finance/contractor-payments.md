# Contractor Payments

**Owner:** Joel (Principal) · **Trigger:** Contractor onboarding; monthly payment run on the 1st · **Cadence:** Monthly · **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-26

## Purpose

Pay the bench reliably and on a predictable date. A contractor who is unsure when
they will be paid is a contractor who prioritises someone else's work — and at
Alivio's size, the bench *is* the delivery capacity.

What breaks without it: ad-hoc payments, no record of what was agreed, and a
January scramble to reconstruct 1099s from bank statements.

## Onboarding a contractor — before any work

Nothing starts until every box is checked. Not "starting while we sort the
paperwork."

- [ ] Contractor agreement signed — scope of relationship, IP assignment,
      confidentiality
- [ ] **IP assignment explicitly included.** Without it Alivio cannot transfer
      ownership to a client, which is a promise the SOW makes
- [ ] W-9 (US) or equivalent, collected **before the first payment**, not in January
- [ ] Rate agreed in writing: hourly or per-project, and what is included
- [ ] Payment method and details
- [ ] Invoice format and submission deadline agreed
- [ ] Added to the contractor register with archetype, rate, and start date
- [ ] Tool access provisioned per `../04-team/onboarding-path.md`

## Rates

Held in the contractor register, not here — rates are individual and change.

Standing rules:

- Rates are set by Joel, in writing, before work
- **Rate changes are prospective only**, never retroactive
- Project rates state exactly what is included and how many revision rounds
- **A contractor's rate is confidential between them and Joel**

## The payment run

**Monthly, on the 1st, for the prior month.** One date, always.

1. Contractors submit invoices by the **25th** for work through month end
2. Joel reconciles each against Linear — hours logged, deliverables accepted
3. Anything disputed is resolved before the run, not held over silently
4. Payment run executes on the 1st
5. Log against the project so **project profitability is computable**

**Late submission means next month's run.** Stated at onboarding so it is a known
rule rather than a punishment.

## Cash rule

**Never pay contractors from money not yet received, unless Joel decides to
deliberately.**

The 40% deposit exists partly to fund the front of the engagement. When a client
invoice is overdue and a contractor payment is due on the same work, that is a
Joel decision with the cash position in front of him — not a default.

This is the mechanism by which an agency dies while looking busy: paying the bench
on time out of reserves while clients pay late, until the reserve is gone.

## Records to keep

- Signed agreement and W-9 per contractor
- Every invoice received
- Every payment made, with date, amount, method, and project
- Annual total per contractor — drives 1099 issuance

## Decision rights

- **Joel decides alone:** rates, who is on the bench, when payments run, any
  advance or early payment.
- **PM decides alone:** which contractor gets which task inside an agreed budget.
- **Escalate when:** a contractor disputes an amount, or delivery requires someone
  not yet onboarded.

## Definition of done

Every contractor is paid on the 1st for the prior month, every payment is logged
against a project, and every contractor has a W-9 on file before their first
payment.

## Assumptions

- **Assumes contractors are US-based 1099** for the W-9 requirement. Alivio's
  observed work spans Colombia, so **international contractors are likely** — they
  need W-8BEN instead, and possibly different withholding. This is a real gap that
  needs an accountant, not a guess.
- Assumes monthly payment is acceptable to the bench. Some contractors require
  faster terms; that is negotiable per contractor but the default should hold.
- Assumes a contractor register exists. **It does not yet** — creating it is a
  prerequisite for this document to function.
