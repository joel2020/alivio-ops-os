# Cash Flow Review

**Owner:** Joel (Principal) · **Trigger:** Weekly, Monday, off the Monday brief · **Cadence:** Weekly · **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-26

## Purpose

A rolling 13-week view of money in and money out, so a shortfall is visible while
there is still time to act on it. Thirteen weeks is the horizon at which an agency
can still change the outcome — inside four weeks the only levers left are painful.

What breaks without it: the cash position is a bank balance checked occasionally,
which reveals a problem at the point where the only remaining options are a
personal loan or a fire-sale discount.

## The 13-week model

One row per week, thirteen weeks forward, rebuilt every Monday.

**Money in:**
- Invoices issued and unpaid, at their **due date** — not their hoped-for date
- Invoices scheduled but not yet issued, from SOW milestone schedules
- Retainers, monthly, at their billing date
- **Weighted pipeline is excluded.** Cash planning uses money that has been agreed,
  not money that has been forecast

**Money out:**
- Contractor payment run, monthly on the 1st
- Recurring tools and subscriptions
- Tax set-aside, quarterly
- Any known one-off

**Each week shows:** opening balance, in, out, closing balance.

## The trigger levels

Act at the threshold, not at zero.

| Level | Condition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 **Comfortable** | Projected low point stays above **8 weeks of operating costs** | Normal operations |
| 🟡 **Watch** | Low point falls to **4–8 weeks** | AR chase becomes top priority; defer non-essential spend; prospecting is a scheduled block |
| 🟠 **Act** | Low point falls to **2–4 weeks** | Chase everything overdue personally; delay non-critical contractor work; consider deposit-heavy terms on new deals |
| 🔴 **Critical** | Low point falls **below 2 weeks** | Stop discretionary spend; Joel personally on every overdue invoice; consider factoring or a credit line — decided in advance, not in the moment |

**The most important number is the projected low point, not today's balance.** A
healthy balance with a $30K contractor run and no receipts due in week six is not
healthy.

## The weekly ritual — 10 minutes, Monday

1. Update the opening balance from the actual bank position
2. Add invoices issued in the last week at their due dates
3. Remove invoices paid
4. Update overdue invoices — **move them to a realistic date, not the original
   due date.** An invoice 20 days overdue does not arrive tomorrow because it is
   supposed to
5. Recompute the projected low point
6. Check it against the trigger levels
7. If the level changed, write down why

**Step 4 is where most cash models lie.** Optimism about overdue invoices is what
makes a model reassuring and useless.

## Retainers versus projects

- **Retainers** are the stable base. Track monthly recurring revenue as a separate
  number — it is what makes the business predictable
- **Projects** are lumpy. A month with three deposits looks great and says nothing
  about the next one
- **Target:** recurring revenue covering fixed operating costs. Then a slow project
  month is uncomfortable rather than dangerous
