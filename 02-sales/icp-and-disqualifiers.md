# ICP and Disqualifiers

**Owner:** Joel (Principal) · **Trigger:** Every inbound lead · **Cadence:** Screened per lead; list reviewed quarterly · **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-26

## Purpose

Most agency pain is sold, not delivered. The bad month traces back to a client who
should have been declined — and at 4–6 concurrent clients, one wrong fit consumes
a fifth of Alivio's capacity for two months.

What breaks without it: Joel decides fit by instinct each time, which is both
slower and inconsistent, and cannot be delegated when a second person joins.

## Who Alivio is for

**Firm fit — pursue actively:**

- **Operator-owners** — founder, principal, or a director with budget authority
  who can decide in one meeting
- **Company size** roughly 5–100 people. Big enough to have a budget, small enough
  that the buyer is the user
- **A problem with a number attached** — revenue they are not capturing, a cost
  they are carrying, a launch with a date
- **Budget of $8K+** for project work, or $2K+/month for retainer
- **Timeline of 4+ weeks.** Anything faster is either trivial or a rescue
- **Comfortable working in writing**, async, across time zones
- **Has tried something already.** A client who has failed once knows the problem
  is real and values the fix

**Strong signals — move faster:**

- Referred by a past client or a trusted network
- Already using modern tooling; not asking Alivio to work inside a system nobody
  can inspect
- Asks what it costs early, without flinching
- Names their decision-maker unprompted
- Talks about outcomes rather than deliverables

## Disqualifiers — say no

Any one is sufficient. A contractor can decline on these without asking.

- **No named decision-maker**, or a buying committee with no owner
- **Budget under $5K** for project work
- **Timeline under 3 weeks** for anything substantial
- **Wants spec work**, a free pilot, or payment in exposure/equity-only
- **Cannot articulate the outcome** — wants "a website" with no reason attached
- **Rescue work without a paid audit first.** Alivio does not fix a system it has
  not inspected, at a fixed price
- **Requires on-site presence** or synchronous availability in an incompatible zone
- **Wants Alivio inside their tooling** where the work cannot be inspected or
  handed over
- **Asked for a discount before hearing the price**
- **Was rude to Joel, or about a previous vendor,** in the first conversation. The
  best predictor of how a client will treat Alivio is how they describe the last
  agency
- **Regulated work Alivio is not qualified for** — medical claims, financial
  advice, legal advice — unless the client owns compliance in writing
- **Anything Alivio cannot say the name of afterwards**

## The three-question screen

Fast enough to run from an inbound email:

1. **Is there one person who can say yes?** No → disqualified.
2. **Is there a number attached to the problem?** No → probably not qualified yet.
3. **Would Alivio want this in the portfolio?** No → decline; capacity is the
   scarce resource.

## Declining well

A good decline is worth more than a bad client. Within 1 business day, in writing,
with the real reason where it is sayable, and a referral where one is obvious. Log
it against the disqualifier that applied.

Every quarter, review the decline reasons. If one disqualifier is firing constantly,
either the marketing is attracting the wrong people or the disqualifier is wrong.

## Decision rights

- **Joel decides alone:** the ICP, the disqualifier list, and any exception to it.
- **Anyone decides alone:** declining a lead that clearly hits a disqualifier.
  Nobody needs permission to say no to a bad fit.
- **Escalate when:** a lead hits a disqualifier but seems worth it anyway. That is
  Joel's call, and it gets logged — repeated exceptions mean the list is wrong.

## Definition of done

Every lead has a fit decision within 1 business day of the qualification call, and
every decline has a logged reason.

## Assumptions

- **The $5K floor and $8K target are derived, not confirmed** — from RLTRS at
  $29,000 and Alivio's published $5–10K install pricing. Joel must set the real
  floor; too low and capacity goes to work that cannot pay for itself.
- The 5–100 employee band is inferred from the observed client set (RLTRS, Bravo
  Mechanical, itslitneon — all small operator-led businesses).
- Assumes Alivio wants to stay generalist across four service lines. **Narrowing to
  one or two would raise prices and shorten sales cycles**, but that is a strategy
  decision, not a process one, and it is not made here.
