# Proposal and Pricing

**Owner:** Joel (Principal) · **Trigger:** Deal enters Proposal stage · **Cadence:** Per deal · **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-26

## Purpose

Price consistently enough that Alivio does not quote the same work two different
ways in the same month, and protect margin from the discount that gets given
because a call felt awkward.

What breaks without it: price becomes a function of how the conversation went,
which teaches clients to negotiate and teaches Alivio to under-price.

## Pricing guardrails by service line

**Floors are hard. Below a floor requires Joel's explicit decision and a written
reason.**

| Service line | Floor | Typical | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AI builds** | $12,000 | $15–30K+ | Highest variance; scope depends on integration surface |
| **Web development** | $8,000 | $10–25K | Platform work sits at the top |
| **Design** | $5,000 | $6–15K | Brand systems at the top; single artefacts at the floor |
| **Marketing** | $3,000 | $3–10K | Often better as retainer |
| **Retainer (any line)** | $2,000/mo | $2–5K/mo | 3-month minimum term |

## How to price

1. **Estimate the hours honestly**, then add 30% for review cycles, coordination,
   and the things nobody estimates. That 30% is not padding; it is the part of
   every project that is real and unbudgeted.
2. **Check it against the floor.** Under the floor is a scope problem, not a price
   problem — reduce scope rather than the number.
3. **Price the outcome, not the hours**, where the outcome has a number attached.
   Hours are what it costs Alivio; they are not what it is worth to the client.
   **Alivio quotes fixed prices only** — hours are an internal costing input and
   never appear in a client document.
4. **Quote one number** for fixed scope. A range invites negotiation to the bottom
   of the range.
5. **Offer at most two options.** One recommended, one smaller. Three options is a
   decision-making burden that delays the close.

## Discount authority

- **Joel only.** Nobody else discusses price.
- **Never a bare discount.** Price moves when scope moves. A discount with no
  scope change tells the client the first number was invented.
- **Acceptable trades:** faster payment terms, a longer commitment, a case study
  and a named reference, a referral introduction, flexible timing that fills a gap.
- **Maximum: 15% off the typical band**, and never below the floor.

## What is never promised without sign-off

Directly from the charter, and repeated here because this is where it gets
promised:

- A date that has not been confirmed with whoever will do the work
- Any deliverable not in the SOW
- More than two revision rounds
- Ongoing support after handoff, unless a retainer is signed
- A specific business result — traffic, revenue, ranking, conversion. Alivio
  promises work, not outcomes it does not control

## Proposal structure

Short. A 20-page proposal is a document nobody reads and a signal Alivio is
selling rather than solving.

1. What you told us — their problem, their words. Proves listening.
2. What we will do — countable deliverables
3. What we will not do — the exclusions list
4. How it works — stages, timeline, checkpoints
5. What we need from you — inputs, owners, dates
6. Price and terms — one number, payment structure, Net 7
7. What happens next — one sentence, one action

Send the SOW as the proposal where possible. Two documents means two review cycles
and two chances to stall.

## Follow-up after sending

Per `follow-up-cadence.md`. **The proposal is sent, then followed up on a schedule
— it is never sent and waited on.** A proposal with no scheduled follow-up is a
proposal that will go quiet.

## Decision rights

- **Joel decides alone:** every price, every discount, every term.
- **Nobody else discusses price with a client.** PM redirects to Joel.
- **Escalate when:** n/a.

## Definition of done

Every proposal has one number, a payment structure, an exclusions list, and a
scheduled follow-up before it is sent.

## Assumptions

- **Every number on this page is a best-practice guess anchored to two observed
  data points** — RLTRS at $29,000 and Alivio's published $5–10K install / $2–3K
  retainer. This is the highest-damage assumption in the entire OS: quoting from an
  invented floor either loses winnable work or wins unprofitable work, and both are
  invisible until the quarter closes. **Joel must set these before anyone quotes.**
- The 30% estimation buffer is a standard services heuristic, not Alivio's measured
  overrun. Track actual versus estimated for a quarter and replace it.
- **Alivio does not bill hourly** (Joel, 2026-07-27). Fixed price for defined
  scope, always, including change orders. The estimation buffer therefore
  matters more than it would at an hourly shop: a short estimate cannot be
  recovered on the invoice, so the 30% is protection rather than padding.
