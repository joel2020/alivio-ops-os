# Win/Loss Capture

**Owner:** PM (runs it) · Joel accountable · **Trigger:** Deal closes Won or Lost; project reaches Delivered · **Cadence:** Within 5 business days · **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-26

## Purpose

Two minutes, captured consistently, that compound into knowing why Alivio wins and
loses. Most agencies never do this because it feels like paperwork on a day that is
already emotional — which is exactly why the version here is short enough to
survive contact with a busy week.

What breaks without it: the same mistake is made four times because nobody
remembers it was a pattern rather than bad luck.

## Deal post-mortem — 2 minutes

Every deal, Won or Lost, within 5 business days. Four fields:

1. **Outcome** — Won / Lost
2. **Primary reason** — one, from the list below. Not three
3. **What would have changed it** — one sentence
4. **Anything to change in the OS** — usually nothing, and that is fine

### Loss reasons — pick exactly one

Constraining the list is what makes the data usable:

- **Price** — went with someone cheaper
- **Timing** — real interest, wrong moment
- **Scope fit** — wanted something Alivio does not do
- **Trust** — chose a more established or better-referenced option
- **Went internal** — decided to build it themselves
- **Went silent** — never got a real decision
- **We declined** — hit a disqualifier
- **Lost to incumbent** — existing vendor kept it

### Win reasons — pick exactly one

- **Referral or reputation**
- **Speed of response**
- **Understood the problem best**
- **Price**
- **Specific capability** — Alivio could do something others could not
- **Existing relationship**

## Project post-mortem — 10 minutes

At Delivered, on every project, run by the PM.

- [ ] Did it land inside the estimated hours? By how much?
- [ ] How many revision rounds actually happened, against two included?
- [ ] How many scope changes, at what total value, and how many went unbilled?
- [ ] Did any milestone slip, and what actually caused it?
- [ ] Did the project go amber or red, and was it caught early?
- [ ] What would we do differently?
- [ ] What did the client say — verbatim, good or bad?
- [ ] Would we take this client again? *If no, why did we take them?*

That last question is the valuable one. Trace it back to the qualification call —
usually the signal was present and got rationalised away.

## Where it goes

- Deal post-mortems: a comment on the deal issue in Linear, plus a running
  `win-loss-log` note in Obsidian
- Project post-mortems: Obsidian, in the client's folder, linked from the decision
  log

## The quarterly read

Once a quarter, read all of them together. Individual post-mortems are anecdotes;
the pattern is the product. Look for:

- The most common loss reason — that is the biggest single lever available
- Whether estimation is systematically optimistic, and by how much
- Whether revision rounds exceed two regularly — a scoping problem, not a client
  problem
- Whether the same disqualifier keeps getting waived

Commit to **one or two changes** per quarter. A list of twelve improvements is a
list nobody executes.

## Decision rights

- **PM decides alone:** running the retro, what goes in it.
- **Joel decides alone:** what changes in the OS as a result.
- **Escalate when:** a post-mortem reveals a systemic problem rather than a
  one-off.

## Definition of done

Every closed deal and every delivered project has a post-mortem within 5 business
days, with exactly one primary reason recorded.

## Assumptions

- Assumes deals close often enough for patterns to appear. At 4–6 concurrent
  clients on 4–8 week cycles that is perhaps 15–25 deals a year — enough for
  annual patterns, thin for quarterly ones. Read quarterly, conclude annually.
- **The 2-minute limit is deliberate.** A thorough post-mortem that never gets done
  is worth less than a shallow one that always does.
