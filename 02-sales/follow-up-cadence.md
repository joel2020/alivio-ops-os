# Follow-Up Cadence

**Owner:** Joel (Principal) · **Trigger:** Proposal sent, or a lead goes quiet · **Cadence:** Per the schedule below · **Last reviewed:** 2026-07-26

## Purpose

Most deals are lost to silence, not to a competitor. The follow-up is where deals
are actually won, and it is the first thing that gets dropped when delivery is busy
— which is precisely when the pipeline most needs it.

Every touch below has a **stated intent**. A follow-up with no purpose beyond
"checking in" trains the client to ignore Alivio, and it is the reason most
follow-up sequences quietly stop working.

## After a proposal is sent

Day 0 is the day the proposal lands.

| Touch | When | Intent | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Day 2** | Make it easy to engage | Value-add. Reference the specific constraint they named, add one useful thought about it. No ask. |
| 2 | **Day 5** | Reduce perceived risk | Relevant proof — a comparable project, what changed, how long it took. |
| 3 | **Day 9** | Surface the real blocker | Direct: *"Is the hold-up scope, timing, or budget?"* Naming the three makes it easy to answer honestly. |
| 4 | **Day 14** | Offer an exit | Breakup: *"Happy to close this out — should I?"* Reliably produces a real answer. |
| — | **Day 15** | — | Mark **Lost** unless the client has given a date. |

**The day-9 message is the one that works.** Most "let me think about it" is one
specific unresolved objection, and naming the three candidates gives permission to
say it.

## After a qualification call with no next step booked

| Touch | When | Intent |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 1 | Recap what was heard, confirm understanding, propose two discovery slots |
| 2 | Day 4 | One useful resource relevant to their problem; repeat the slots |
| 3 | Day 10 | Direct ask: still worth pursuing? |
| — | Day 11 | Mark Lost |

## After a client goes quiet mid-deal

| Touch | When | Intent |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 3 | Assume good faith — short, no pressure, easy to reply to |
| 2 | Day 7 | Change channel. Email → call, or the reverse |
| 3 | Day 12 | Direct: has something changed on your side? |
| — | Day 13 | Mark Lost, with a note on the last known position |

## Long-term nurture

Deals marked Lost for **timing** are worth a touch every 90 days. Not a newsletter
— one message referencing the specific thing they were trying to do. Roughly one
in five reopens, and it is the cheapest pipeline available.

Deals lost on **fit** or **price** get no nurture. Re-approaching them teaches
nothing and costs goodwill.

## Rules

- **Every touch adds something.** No bare "just following up" — it is a message
  that says Alivio wants something and offers nothing.
- **Vary the channel.** Three unanswered emails means email is not working.
- **Never more than four touches** before Lost. Persistence past that reads as
  desperation and damages the brand more than the deal is worth.
- **Always leave the door open.** The breakup message is warm. People change jobs
  and take vendors with them.
- **Log every touch** in the deal. An unlogged follow-up will be repeated or
  forgotten, and both are visible to the client.

## Decision rights

- **Joel decides alone:** all of it. Sales is Joel's.
- **Escalate when:** n/a.

## Definition of done

No open deal is more than 5 business days past its last touch, and every deal past
its final touch is marked Lost.

## Assumptions

- **The day intervals are standard B2B services practice, not Alivio's measured
  results.** They are a reasonable starting point; replace them once there is data
  on which touch actually converts.
- Assumes a 4–8 week delivery cycle and correspondingly quick decisions. Longer
  enterprise cycles need wider spacing.
- Assumes Joel writes each touch. **These are strong candidates for the Phase 3
  `alivio-chase`-style drafting** — drafted automatically, sent only by Joel.
